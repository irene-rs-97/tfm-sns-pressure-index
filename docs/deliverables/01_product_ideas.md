# Delivery 1 – Product Ideas

*(Formal, academic and technical English version)*

## Context

This is the first submission of the final project for the Master's in Data Science and Artificial Intelligence (Evolve). The objective is to propose at least three project ideas connected to real-world problems, describing the issue they address, their potential impact, and the personal motivation for developing them.

---

## Idea 1: Application for Locating Pharmacies Based on Product Availability

### Problem Addressed

Individuals frequently need to visit multiple pharmacies to locate a specific medication or over-the-counter product, particularly when urgency is involved. This situation leads to time loss, unnecessary travel, and, in certain cases, delays in accessing the required treatment.

### Potential Impact or Value

Developing an application that enables users to check product availability across nearby pharmacies could substantially improve accessibility and efficiency in acquiring medications. Beyond optimizing users' time, such a system would support a more balanced distribution of demand among establishments, reducing pressure on specific locations and promoting more efficient use of available resources.

### Personal Motivation

This proposal is especially compelling because it integrates technology with everyday healthcare services, addressing a concrete and easily identifiable societal need. It offers a practical solution to real situations, particularly in urgent contexts or when dealing with products that are difficult to locate.

## Idea 2: Hospital Guidance Application Based on Occupancy Levels

### Problem Addressed

During certain periods—such as epidemics, seasonal peaks, or weekends—some hospitals experience significantly higher saturation levels than others. This can result in prolonged waiting times and increased pressure on healthcare personnel.

### Potential Impact or Value

An application providing real-time information on hospital occupancy levels could facilitate a more efficient distribution of non-critical patients, reducing waiting times and improving the overall care experience. Additionally, it could serve as a decision-support tool for planning and managing healthcare resources more effectively.

### Personal Motivation

This idea addresses a structural challenge within healthcare systems: the efficient management of resources during periods of high demand. It combines data analysis with direct social impact, reinforcing both its relevance and potential utility.

## Idea 3: Intelligent Scoring System for Evaluating Hospital Discharge and Transfer Criteria

*(Selected for Delivery 2)*

### Problem Addressed

Premature discharges and avoidable readmissions constitute some of the most frequent and costly issues within healthcare systems. When a hospital operates under high pressure, discharge decisions may be influenced by the need to free up beds, potentially leading to unnecessary readmissions with worse clinical outcomes and higher system-level costs. Similarly, patient transfers between hospitals are often reactive—occurring once capacity limits have already been reached—rather than anticipatory. Both issues share a common root: the absence of data-driven tools that support these decisions objectively, consistently, and proactively.

### Potential Impact or Value

Developing an intelligent system that integrates a readmission-risk scoring model with a hospital-transfer algorithm based on occupancy levels and patient clinical profiles could significantly reduce avoidable readmissions, optimize bed utilization, and improve equity in clinical decision-making. According to INE 2023 data, between 20–25% of 30-day readmissions in high-prevalence chronic conditions such as heart failure are potentially avoidable, representing estimated savings exceeding €55 million annually for that diagnostic category alone. Furthermore, the 283,792 unplanned transfers recorded in 2023 highlight the human and economic cost of reactive, rather than planned and data-driven, patient transfers.

### Personal Motivation

This project aligns directly with my interest in applying advanced analytics to improve complex healthcare systems. The combination of predictive modelling, multicriteria optimization, and data visualization applied to a problem with direct implications for patient safety represents precisely the type of challenge that motivates this work. Additionally, access to high-quality public data sources—such as the INE Hospital Morbidity Survey and the ESCRI statistics from the Ministry of Health—makes the project viable and grounded in real data from the Spanish healthcare system.
